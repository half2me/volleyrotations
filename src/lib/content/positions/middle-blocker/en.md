# Playing middle blocker

Middle blockers (also called middles or centres) live at the net. You lead the block, read the opposing setter, and run quick attacks in front of or behind your own setter. A 5-1 has two middles, opposite each other in the rotation, so one is always in the front row.

## Every moment of the rally

- **Serve receive, front row:** you usually do not pass. Wait at the net, out of the passers' way, then back off to approach for a quick attack.
- **Back row:** on most teams the libero comes on for you. Under FIVB rules you still come back to **serve** when you rotate to zone 1, because the libero may not serve there; you stay on until your team loses the serve, then the libero replaces you again.
- **Defense:** read their setter and move along the net to join the block on either side. Close the gap to your outside blocker so the ball cannot go between you.
- **Attack:** approach for the quick set in front of the setter, or the slide behind them. Even when you are not set, your approach holds their middle blocker.
- **Cover:** after your approach, if the ball goes elsewhere, turn and cover.

## Rules that matter most to you

- Touching the net between the antennas while playing the ball is a fault ([rule 11.3](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=33)).
- Libero replacements do not count as substitutions, but there must be a completed rally between two of them ([rule 19.3.2](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=45)).
- Some leagues let the libero serve in one position; see [rules by league](/guides/leagues).

## Common mistakes

- Jumping with the hitter's approach instead of the set: watch the setter's hands, then the ball.
- Leaving a gap between you and the outside blocker.
- Approaching too late to be a real threat on the quick.
- Forgetting to come on to serve, or leaving the court the wrong way: replacements happen at the replacement zone by the bench.

# Playing outside hitter

Outside hitters (also called left side or wing spikers) do a bit of everything: they pass in every rotation, attack from the left in the front row, and often hit the pipe from the back row. A 5-1 has two, standing opposite each other, so one is always in the front row.

## Every moment of the rally

- **Serve receive:** you are one of the main passers with the libero, in every rotation. Front row, pass on the side you will attack from when the overlap rule allows; then get off the net to start your approach.
- **When your team serves:** serve, then run to your defensive spot. In this site's tools that is middle back (zone 6) in the back row, and the left side at the net in the front row.
- **Defense:** in the front row, block on the left against their opposite, or drop off to dig if you are not blocking. In the back row, dig deep.
- **Attack:** in the front row, hit from the left. In rotation 1 many teams let the outside who passed on the right hit from the right for that first attack. In the back row, attack the pipe in the middle, taking off from behind the attack line.
- **Cover:** when someone else attacks, get low around them.

## Rules that matter most to you

- From the back row you may attack a ball above the net only if you take off from behind the attack line; landing in front of it afterwards is fine ([rule 13](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=36)).
- When your team receives serve, you must keep your rotational order with the players beside and in front of or behind you ([rule 7.4](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=27)), even when passing in an unusual spot.

## Common mistakes

- Passing and approaching from the same spot: after the pass you need distance from the net to approach.
- Starting the approach too early, before the set is out of the setter's hands.
- Forgetting the pipe or the cover in the back row and standing still.
- Stepping into a teammate's passing area instead of calling the ball early.

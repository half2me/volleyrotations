# How volleyball rotation works

<Callout>

**In short**

- Each side of the court has six zones. Seen from behind your own end line: front row **4, 3, 2** (left to right), back row **5, 6, 1**. The player in zone 1 serves.
- When your team wins a rally on the opponent's serve (a **sideout**), you rotate one place **clockwise** and the player who moves into zone 1 serves.
- The order never changes during a set. It decides who serves and who is front or back row.
- "Rotation 1" to "Rotation 6" name the six line-ups by where the setter stands: zone 1, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2.
- Rotation only fixes where you stand at the serve. After the serve is hit, players **switch** to their specialty spots, within the front-row and back-row limits.

</Callout>

## The six zones

|                            | Left | Middle | Right |
| -------------------------- | ---- | ------ | ----- |
| **Front row** (at the net) | 4    | 3      | 2     |
| **Back row**               | 5    | 6      | 1     |

Picture yourself standing behind your own end line, looking at the net. That is how this site draws every court: the net at the top. Zone 1 is right back, the serving position. The zones are numbered in [FIVB rule 7.4.1](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=27), and the numbering follows the order in which players rotate.

The attack line, 3 m from the centre line, splits each half into a front zone and a back zone. Note that the attack line is not what makes you "front row": that depends only on your place in the rotation.

## Service order and the starting line-up

Before each set the coach hands in a line-up that fixes the order of the six starters ([rule 7.3](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=26)). That order stays the same for the whole set. If your team serves first, the player in zone 1 serves first. Every later server is simply whoever rotates into zone 1.

## When you rotate

You rotate only when your team **wins back the right to serve**: the opponent served, and your team won the rally. Then every player moves one place clockwise ([rule 7.6.2](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=28)):

- 2 → 1 (this player now serves)
- 1 → 6
- 6 → 5
- 5 → 4
- 4 → 3
- 3 → 2

If your team serves and wins the rally, the same server serves again and nobody moves. If you lose a rally on your own serve, the other team rotates, not you.

Over a set your team goes round and round the same six line-ups.

## Rotation 1 to 6

Coaches name the six line-ups by where the setter stands at the serve. On this site, and on most teams:

| Name       | Setter in zone | Setter is         |
| ---------- | -------------- | ----------------- |
| Rotation 1 | 1              | back row, serving |
| Rotation 2 | 6              | back row          |
| Rotation 3 | 5              | back row          |
| Rotation 4 | 4              | front row         |
| Rotation 5 | 3              | front row         |
| Rotation 6 | 2              | front row         |

The numbers follow the order of play: after Rotation 1 the team rotates into Rotation 2, and so on. Some teams count in a different direction or name rotations after the server instead, so check what your coach means.

In a [5-1 system](/guides/systems), Rotation 1 typically looks like this: setter in 1, outside hitter in 2, middle blocker in 3, opposite in 4, the other outside hitter in 5, the other middle in 6.

## Switching after the serve

Rotation only governs the moment the serve is hit. As soon as the server's hand touches the ball, every player may go anywhere on their own court ([rule 7.4.4](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=27)). Teams use this to put players where they are strongest:

- **Front row:** outside hitter to the left, middle blocker to the middle, setter or opposite to the right.
- **Back row:** setter or opposite to right back (zone 1), outside hitter to middle back (zone 6, ready for the pipe), libero to left back (zone 5).

Before the serve, players stand as close to their target spot as the [overlap rule](/guides/overlap) allows, then switch the moment the ball is hit. On your own serve, the switch happens as soon as the server contacts the ball. On the opponent's serve, most players pass first and switch on the transition.

## Front row and back row limits

Switching does not change who is front row. At the serve, zones 4, 3 and 2 are front row and zones 5, 6 and 1 are back row, and that holds for the whole rally:

- A back-row player may **not block** or take part in a completed block ([rule 14.6](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=37)).
- A back-row player may **attack a ball that is entirely above the net** only if they take off from behind the attack line. They may land in front of it ([rule 13.2](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=35)). From in front of the line they may only play a ball that is at least partly below the top of the net.
- The libero has stricter limits again: see [the libero guide](/guides/libero).

## Common questions

**Does the libero rotate?** The libero replaces a back-row player and takes that player's place in the order. When that player would rotate to the front row (zone 4), the libero must leave and the original player comes back on.

**What if we serve out of order?** That is a rotational fault ([rule 7.7](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=28)): the opponent gets the point and the serve, and the order is corrected. See [substitutions and rules](/guides/substitutions).

**Do we rotate at the start of a set?** No. The coach can hand in a new order for each set, so the starting positions can change between sets.

# Playing opposite

The opposite (also called right side, or opposite hitter) stands directly across the rotation from the setter, so exactly one of you is in the front row at any time. You attack from the right, block their best hitter, and are often the team's top scorer because you usually do not pass.

## Every moment of the rally

- **Serve receive:** usually you do not pass. Wait where the overlap rule puts you, out of the passers' way, often near the right side so you can approach. In a four-player receive, or in a rotation where a front-row outside has to shield the setter, you may pass too.
- **When your team serves:** serve, then go to right back in the back row, or the right side at the net in the front row.
- **Defense:** in the front row, block on the right against their outside hitter, who usually gets the most sets. In the back row, defend right back.
- **Attack:** in the front row, hit from the right. In rotation 1 the opposite often starts the first attack from the left, because the outside who passed on the right hits from there. In the back row, attack from right back (zone 1), taking off from behind the attack line.
- **Cover:** cover your hitters when the set goes elsewhere.

## Rules that matter most to you

- From the back row you may attack a ball above the net only if you take off from behind the attack line ([rule 13](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=36)).
- As a back-row player you may not complete a block ([rule 14.6](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=37)).

## Common mistakes

- Starting the approach from too close to the net, with no room to jump.
- Blocking too far inside and leaving the line open against their outside hitter.
- Standing still in the back row instead of being an attack option from zone 1.
- Drifting into the passers' area in serve receive when you are not passing.

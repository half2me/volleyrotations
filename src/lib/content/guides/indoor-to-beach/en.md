# From indoor to beach volleyball

<Callout>

**In short**

- On the beach you play **two against two** on an **8 × 8 m** half court. There are no positions, no rotation and no overlap rule: both players pass, set and attack.
- Only the **serve order** is fixed. When your team wins the serve back, the player who did not serve last time serves.
- The **open-hand tip is a fault**, the **block counts as one of your three touches**, and hand-setting is judged more strictly.
- Sets go to **21** (the third to 15), you **switch sides every 7 points**, and at FIVB World and Official competitions **no one coaches you** during the match.
- Expect to jump lower and move more slowly on sand, and to allow for the wind and sun in every rally.

</Callout>

## What stays the same

The net is the same height (2.43 m for men, 2.24 m for women in senior play), you still have three hits per side, every rally scores a point, and a set must be won by two. Your passing, serving and hitting all carry over. What changes is that you can no longer hide: a beach player has to do everything.

## The game in numbers

|                  | Indoor                                           | Beach                                                                      |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Players per side | 6 (plus substitutes and libero)                  | 2, no substitutes                                                          |
| Court per side   | 9 × 9 m                                          | 8 × 8 m                                                                    |
| Set length       | 25 points (fifth set 15)                         | 21 points (third set 15)                                                   |
| Match            | best of five, in FIVB play                       | best of three                                                              |
| Change sides     | after sets 1–3, and at 8 points in the fifth set | every 7 points played (every 5 in the third set)                           |
| Time-outs        | 2 per set, 30 s each                             | 1 per set, plus a technical time-out at 21 total points in sets 1–2 (FIVB) |
| Serve time       | 8 seconds after the whistle                      | 5 seconds after the whistle                                                |
| Ball             | 65–67 cm, 0.300–0.325 kg/cm²                     | 66–68 cm, 0.175–0.225 kg/cm²: softer and slower                            |

Each beach player covers 32 m² of court, against about 13.5 m² indoors. See [the court, net and ball](/equipment) drawn to scale, and [beach vs indoor](/beach) for every difference with its rule.

## No positions, no rotation

There are no zones to learn and no overlap faults: at the serve, each team only has to be inside its own court, the server excepted ([beach rule 7.4–7.5](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/FIVB-BeachVolleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v01.pdf#page=23)). Pairs still organise themselves:

- **Receiving sides.** Most pairs split the court left and right, and each player usually passes on the same side every rally.
- **Blocker and defender.** Many pairs have a regular blocker, usually the taller player, and a regular defender who stays back. Others split the job: the player who is not serving blocks.
- **Pass, set, hit.** Whoever passes usually attacks, and the partner sets. Expect to attack after most of your passes.

## Serve order

The serve order is set at the start of each set and kept until the set ends ([beach rule 7.6](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/FIVB-BeachVolleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v01.pdf#page=23)). When your team wins the serve back, the player who did **not** serve last time serves; while you keep winning rallies, the same player keeps serving ([12.2](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/FIVB-BeachVolleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v01.pdf#page=28)). Serving out of order costs a point and the serve ([7.7](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/FIVB-BeachVolleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v01.pdf#page=23)).

You have **5 seconds** after the whistle to serve, not 8 ([12.4.4](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/FIVB-BeachVolleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v01.pdf#page=29)). Your partner must not screen the receivers' view of the server, and no player of the serving team may raise their hands above their head until the ball has crossed the net ([12.5](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/FIVB-BeachVolleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v01.pdf#page=29)).

## Rules that catch indoor players

**No open-hand tip.** Tipping with open fingers is an attack fault on the beach ([13.2.3](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/FIVB-BeachVolleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v01.pdf#page=30)). Players tip with their knuckles (a "pokey") or with rigid fingertips held together (a "cobra"), or hit a roll shot or cut shot instead.

**Setting over the net.** If you attack with an overhand pass, the ball must travel at right angles to the line of your shoulders, unless you were trying to set your partner ([13.2.5](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/FIVB-BeachVolleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v01.pdf#page=30)). A sideways set over the net is a fault.

**Hand-setting is judged strictly.** Beach referees tend to call doubles and held balls more often than indoor ones. Indoors, a double contact on your team's first hit is allowed in one action; on the beach it is not when the first hit is played overhand with the fingers ([9.2.2.2](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/FIVB-BeachVolleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v01.pdf#page=26)). When you dig a hard-driven ball, an overhand contact may last a moment longer ([9.2.2.4](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/FIVB-BeachVolleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v01.pdf#page=26)). Many beach players pass the serve with their forearms only.

**The block is one of your three touches.** After a block touch your team has only two more hits, and the blocker may take the next one ([14.4](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/FIVB-BeachVolleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v01.pdf#page=31)). Indoors the block does not count.

**No centre line.** You may cross under the net into the opponent's court, as long as you do not interfere with their play ([11.2.1](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/FIVB-BeachVolleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v01.pdf#page=27)). Touching the net between the antennae while playing the ball is still a fault.

**Attacking the serve.** You may not attack the opponent's serve while the ball is entirely above the top of the net, anywhere on the court ([13.2.4](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/FIVB-BeachVolleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v01.pdf#page=30)). Blocking the serve is forbidden in both games.

**No coach.** At FIVB World and Official competitions no one may coach you during the match; age-group events may allow it ([4.1.4](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/FIVB-BeachVolleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v01.pdf#page=18)). You and your partner decide tactics, time-outs and fixes on your own, so talk between rallies.

**One time-out, and no substitutions.** Each team has one 30-second time-out per set ([15.1](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/FIVB-BeachVolleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v01.pdf#page=32)). An injured player has up to 5 minutes to recover; if they cannot carry on, the team is declared incomplete and loses the match ([17.1.2](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/FIVB-BeachVolleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v01.pdf#page=34)).

## Sand, wind and sun

- **Footwork.** You will jump lower and move more slowly. Take shorter, quicker steps, and start moving earlier.
- **Wind.** The wind moves the ball, so allow for it when you serve and set; many players set lower in strong wind. Switching sides every 7 points means both teams play with and against it.
- **Sun.** Know which side has the sun in your eyes. Some players serve high into the sun to make the pass harder.
- **The ball** is softer and floats more. Float serves can move more, and hard-driven balls are slower than indoors.

## How to start

1. **Find a partner** and agree who receives on which side, and who blocks.
2. **Learn the bump set.** Forearm sets are common on the beach because they are safer than hand sets with strict refereeing.
3. **Learn two or three shots:** the cut shot, the line shot and the pokey. Most beach points are won with placement, not power.
4. **Learn the hand signals** your partner shows behind their back before the serve: [beach partner hand signals](/beach).
5. **Call everything:** "Mine!", where to set, the line or angle, and "Ball!" when the opponent attacks.
6. **Keep score with the [match tracker](/match)** in beach mode: it tracks the serve order and tells you when to switch sides.

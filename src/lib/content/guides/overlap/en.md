# The overlap rule in volleyball

<Callout>

**In short**

- At the moment of the serve, players must stand in their rotation order. Breaking it is an **overlap**, officially a **positional fault**, and costs the rally.
- You only compare each player with their **neighbours**: front with back in the same column (4–5, 3–6, 2–1) and left with right within the same row (4–3–2, 5–6–1).
- Position is judged by **feet on the floor**. Being level, or having part of one foot past, is enough.
- Under the FIVB rules for 2025–2028, only the **receiving team** has to be in rotation order. The serving team may stand anywhere on its court. Many leagues, for example NCAA and US high school, still check both teams, so ask yours.
- Once the serve is hit, everyone may go anywhere.

</Callout>

## The rule

[FIVB rule 7.4](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=27) (2025–2028) says that at the moment the server hits the ball, each team must be within its own court (except the server). The players of the **receiving** team must be in rotation order. The players of the **serving** team are free to occupy any position.

For the receiving team, the order means two things:

1. **Front to back** (7.4.2.1): each back-row player must be further from the centre line than the front-row player directly in front of them. The pairs are 4 and 5 (left), 3 and 6 (middle), 2 and 1 (right).
2. **Side to side** (7.4.2.2): within each row the left-to-right order must be kept: 4, 3, 2 in the front row and 5, 6, 1 in the back row.

Breaking either is a positional fault ([rule 7.5](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=27)): the team loses the rally, and the players are put back in the right places.

## How it is judged: feet

Rule 7.4.3 says the positions are judged by the players' feet touching the floor:

- **Front to back:** the back-row player must be level with, or have at least part of one foot further from the centre line than, the front foot of the corresponding front-row player.
- **Side to side:** a right-side player must be level with, or have at least part of one foot closer to the right sideline than, the foot further from that sideline of the other players in the same row. The same works on the left. For the middle player, that simply means being between the two.

In practice that means a back-row player can stand almost touching the heels of the player in front, and two neighbours in a row can stand nearly side by side. Hands, arms and leaning do not count: only feet on the floor.

## Neighbours only

You are only compared with the players next to you in your column and in your row. There are no diagonal checks.

| Player in zone | Must be behind | Must be in front of | Left of | Right of |
| -------------- | -------------- | ------------------- | ------- | -------- |
| 4              |                | 5                   | 3       |          |
| 3              |                | 6                   | 2       | 4        |
| 2              |                | 1                   |         | 3        |
| 5              | 4              |                     | 6       |          |
| 6              | 3              |                     | 1       | 5        |
| 1              | 2              |                     |         | 6        |

So zone 5 and zone 3 are never compared, nor are 4 and 6 or 2 and 6. In practice zone 4 and zone 2 cannot swap as long as each is correct against zone 3. A back-row player can legally stand in front of a front-row player who is not in their own column. That is the key to every serve-receive formation.

## The 2025 change, and leagues that differ

Older FIVB rules applied rotation order to **both** teams at the serve. Since 2025, FIVB only checks the receiving team, and the serving team can line up however it likes, as long as everyone except the server is on the court. It still may not screen: players may not wave their arms, jump, move sideways or stand grouped to hide the server or the flight of the ball, and since 2025 no serving-team player may raise their hands above their head during the serve until the ball has crossed the net ([rule 12.5](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=34)).

Many competitions have not followed, or follow a different rulebook: NCAA college volleyball, US high school (NFHS) and USA Volleyball's domestic rules all still check the serving team's order, as do plenty of local and school leagues. Some national federations adopt FIVB changes a season or more later. **Check which rule your league uses.** In the [overlap trainer](/overlap) you can pick your league and practise the serving team's order too; [the leagues guide](/guides/leagues) compares the rule sets.

## Stacking and hiding players

Teams use the neighbours-only rule to put players close to where they want to be after the serve. This is called **stacking** or **hiding**.

**Example: Rotation 5 in a 5-1** (setter in zone 3, middle blocker in zone 2, opposite in zone 6). The setter wants to be at the target on the right. The setter only has to stay left of the zone 2 player and in front of the zone 6 player. So the middle blocker stands right up against the right sideline at the net, the setter stands just to their left, also at the net, and the opposite stays back. When the serve is hit, the setter takes one step to the target and the middle runs in to attack.

**Example: Rotation 1** (setter in zone 1). The setter must be behind the zone 2 player and right of the zone 6 player. The setter usually stands deep on the right, behind the zone 2 player, and runs forward to the target as the ball is served. Nobody needs the setter to pass, so the other receivers cover the court in front.

<OverlapFigure rotation={1} you="S" caption="Rotation 1: the setter (S, zone 1) stays behind the zone 2 passer and right of the zone 6 passer, deep on the right, then runs to the target. The zone 6 passer only has to be behind the middle at the net." />

**Example: hiding a front-row player.** A middle blocker in zone 3 who should not pass can stand right at the net. The zone 6 player then only has to be slightly deeper than that, so the back-row passers can spread across the court.

## Common mistakes

- **The setter leaves too early.** In Rotation 1 the setter steps forward before the serve and ends up closer to the net than the zone 2 player. Wait for the contact.
- **Swapping sides in the front row.** In a rotation where the outside hitter is in zone 2 and the opposite in zone 4, they may not cross before the serve. Keep the zone 3 player between you at the serve, then switch.
- **Forgetting the libero.** The libero takes the exact place in the order of the player they replaced. If they replaced the zone 5 player, they must be behind zone 4 and left of zone 6.
- **Checking the wrong players.** Players often worry about diagonal neighbours, which do not count, and forget the player directly in front.
- **Judging by the body.** Leaning past a teammate is fine; only the feet count.

## Positional versus rotational fault

A **positional fault** is standing in the wrong place at the serve. A **rotational fault** is serving out of order ([rule 7.7](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=28)). Both cost the rally, but a rotational fault can also wipe out points scored while the order was wrong. See [substitutions and rules](/guides/substitutions).

# From beach to indoor volleyball

<Callout>

**In short**

- Indoors you play **six against six** on a **9 × 9 m** half court (beach: two against two on 8 × 8 m). You cover much less court, but you have to know exactly where to be.
- Everyone has a **specialised position**, and a **fixed rotation order** that decides where you stand at the serve and who serves. Standing out of order is a fault.
- The **open-hand tip is legal** indoors, the **block does not count** as one of your three touches, and hand-setting is judged more leniently.
- There is a **centre line** under the net and an **attack line** that limits back-row players.
- Learn your role in the rotation first. Your ball skills already transfer.

</Callout>

## What stays the same

Your passing, setting, hitting, serving and reading of the game all carry over. Beach players are usually excellent all-rounders: you read hitters well, you dig anything, and you are used to making decisions on your own. Indoor coaches love that. The net heights are the same (2.43 m for men, 2.24 m for women in senior play), and so is rally scoring.

## The court and the team

|                  | Beach                                                                      | Indoor                                           |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Players per side | 2                                                                          | 6 (plus substitutes and libero)                  |
| Court per side   | 8 × 8 m                                                                    | 9 × 9 m                                          |
| Set length       | 21 points (third set 15)                                                   | 25 points (fifth set 15)                         |
| Match            | best of three                                                              | best of five, in FIVB play                       |
| Change sides     | every 7 points (every 5 in the third set)                                  | after each set, and at 8 points in the fifth set |
| Substitutions    | none                                                                       | 6 per set, plus unlimited libero replacements    |
| Time-outs        | 1 per set, plus a technical time-out at 21 total points in sets 1–2 (FIVB) | 2 per set, 30 s each                             |

The indoor court is bigger, but it is shared by six players, so each of you covers a much smaller area. The ball weighs the same, but it is pumped harder and is slightly smaller, so it is firmer and travels faster indoors with no wind.

## Positions: you have one job

On the beach you do everything. Indoors you are usually a specialist:

- **Setter:** takes the second ball every rally.
- **Outside hitter:** passes and attacks from the left. Often the easiest fit for beach players, because it needs passing and all-round attacking.
- **Middle blocker:** blocks and hits quick sets.
- **Opposite:** attacks from the right, and blocks the other team's outside hitter (usually their busiest attacker).
- **Libero:** back-row passer and defender in a different shirt. Strong beach defenders often make good liberos.

Read [the positions guide](/guides/positions) for more.

## Rotation and the overlap rule

This is the biggest change. On the beach you only have to serve in the right order. Indoors:

- The six players have a **fixed order** for the whole set. Each time your team wins back the serve, everyone moves one place **clockwise**, and the player moving into zone 1 serves. See [how rotation works](/guides/rotations).
- At the moment of the serve, the receiving team's players must stand in that order relative to their neighbours: front to back and side to side. Otherwise it is a **positional fault**. See [the overlap rule](/guides/overlap).
- After the serve is hit, everyone **switches** to their specialty spot.
- If you are in the **back row** at the serve, you may not complete a block, and you may only attack a ball that is entirely above the net if you take off from behind the **attack line**, 3 m from the net.

## The libero

The libero comes on for a back-row player, usually a middle blocker, without using a substitution. Under FIVB rules the libero may not serve or block, and may not attack a ball that is entirely above the net. See [the libero guide](/guides/libero).

## Rule differences that catch beach players

**Tipping.** Indoors you may tip with your fingertips, open hand. On the beach, an open-hand tip is a fault, so you learned to use your knuckles (a "pokey"), stiff straight fingertips (a "cobra") or a roll shot. Indoors, use whichever works.

**The block is not a touch.** On the beach a block counts as one of your three contacts. Indoors it does not ([rule 14.4](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=37)): after a block, your team still has three touches, and the blocker may also make the next one.

**Setting.** Beach referees are strict about spin and double contacts on hand sets, and there are extra rules on setting the ball over the net. Indoors, hand-setting is judged more leniently, and there is no rule about setting square over the net. On the team's first contact (receiving the serve, digging), consecutive contacts are allowed indoors as long as they happen during one action. Overhand passing of the serve is common and legal.

**The centre line.** On the beach there is no centre line, and you may go into the opponent's side as long as you do not interfere with them. Indoors there is a centre line under the net. You may touch the opponent's court with your feet only if part of the foot stays in contact with, or directly above, the centre line. Touching the opponent's court with any other part of the body above the feet is allowed only if it does not interfere with their play. Check [the official rules](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=32) for the details.

**The net.** As on the beach, touching the net between the antennae while playing the ball is a fault. In practice, indoor referees see more of it because more players are near the net.

**Attacking the serve.** As on the beach, you may not attack the opponent's serve while it is entirely above the net, but indoors this applies only while the ball is in your front zone ([rule 13.3](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=36)).

**Communication and coaching.** Indoors a coach can give instructions from the bench, call time-outs and make substitutions. On the beach, coaching is limited or not allowed at all, depending on the competition.

## How to learn your role fast

1. **Learn your six rotations.** Ask your coach which position you play, then step through every rotation in the [position explorer](/explorer). Know where you stand in serve receive and where you go after the switch.
2. **Learn the neighbours rule.** In each rotation, know who stands in front of, behind and beside you. That is all you need to avoid an overlap. Practise in the [overlap trainer](/overlap).
3. **Trust the system.** On the beach you chase every ball. Indoors, stay in your area: a teammate is covering the rest. Running into someone else's space leaves your own empty.
4. **Talk.** Six players need more calls than two: "Mine!", "Out!", the attack you want, who takes the tip.
5. **Use your beach strengths.** Reading the hitter, digging, and setting from bad positions are rare indoor skills. Bring them.
6. **Test yourself** with the [rotation quiz](/quiz) before your first match.

# Volleyball positions explained

<Callout>

**In short**

- An indoor team has six players on court: usually one **setter**, two **outside hitters**, two **middle blockers** and one **opposite**, plus a **libero** who comes in for back-row players.
- The setter runs the offence and takes the second touch. The others attack, block, pass and dig.
- Where you stand at the serve is fixed by the rotation. Once the serve is hit, everyone **switches** to their specialty spot.
- Front-row players can attack from anywhere and block. Back-row players may not complete a block, and may only attack a ball that is entirely above the net if they take off from behind the attack line.

</Callout>

## Zones and positions are different things

A **zone** (1 to 6) is where you have to stand when the ball is served, and it changes every time your team rotates. A **position** is the job you do once play starts. A middle blocker is a middle blocker in every rotation, even when the rotation puts them in zone 4 or zone 1.

The court is described from behind your own end line, facing the net: front row 4, 3, 2 from left to right, back row 5, 6, 1. Zone 1 serves. See [how rotation works](/guides/rotations) for the full picture.

In the common **5-1 system** the six starters sit in the rotation so that each player has a partner directly opposite them: setter and opposite, the two outside hitters, the two middle blockers. Because opposite players are always three places apart, one of each pair is in the front row and the other in the back row.

## The setter (S)

- **Attack:** takes the second touch on almost every rally and decides who hits. A good setter delivers a ball that is easy to hit and hard to read. When in the front row the setter may also tip or attack the second ball ("dump").
- **Block:** in the front row, usually blocks on the right side against the opponent's outside hitter.
- **Serve receive:** is normally kept out of receiving. The setter "hides" near the net and runs to the target, which is slightly right of the middle, about a metre off the net.
- **Defense:** in the back row usually defends right back (zone 1), so the run to the target is short.

## Outside hitter (OH)

Also called left side or wing spiker.

- **Attack:** hits from the left side (zone 4) in the front row (in Rotation 1 many teams let them hit from the right for the first attack after serve receive). In the back row, many teams use the outside hitter for the **pipe**, a back-row attack from the middle, jumping from behind the attack line.
- **Block:** left side, against the opponent's opposite.
- **Serve receive:** the main passers together with the libero. Outside hitters have to pass well, because they receive in every rotation.
- **Defense:** in the back row, usually middle back (zone 6) in this site's tools, or left back on some teams.

## Middle blocker (MB)

Also called middle or centre.

- **Attack:** quick, fast sets in the middle (the "quick" or "1") and slides behind the setter. Even when not set, their approach freezes the opponent's middle blocker.
- **Block:** the most important blocker. Reads the setter and moves along the net to join the block on either side.
- **Serve receive:** usually not in the passing formation.
- **Back row:** on most teams the libero replaces the middle blocker in the back row. Under FIVB rules the middle still serves when rotating into zone 1, because the libero may not serve. As soon as the team loses the serve, the libero can come back in for them.

## Opposite (OPP)

Also called right side, or opposite hitter.

- **Attack:** hits from the right side (zone 2) in the front row and often from right back (zone 1) in the back row. Often the team's top scorer, because the opposite does not usually pass.
- **Block:** right side, against the opponent's outside hitter, which is usually their busiest attacker.
- **Serve receive:** usually not in the passing formation.
- **Defense:** usually right back (zone 1) in the back row.
- The opposite stands directly across the rotation from the setter, so there is always exactly one of them in the front row.

## Libero (L)

- A back-row specialist in a different coloured shirt. Comes in for any back-row player without counting as a substitution, usually for the middle blockers.
- Passes and digs; under FIVB rules may not serve, block or attack a ball that is entirely above the net. See [the libero guide](/guides/libero) and [FIVB rule 19](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=44).
- Usually plays left back (zone 5) in defense and takes the biggest share of serve receive.

## Defensive specialist (DS)

A substitute brought in for a back-row player to pass and dig, typically for the opposite or a weak-passing hitter. Unlike the libero, a DS is a normal substitution ([FIVB rule 15.6](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=39)) and may serve, and they wear the same shirt as everyone else. Recreational teams that have no libero often use a DS instead.

## Front row versus back row

|                                      | Front row (zones 4, 3, 2) | Back row (zones 5, 6, 1)                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Block                                | Yes                       | No ([rule 14.6](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=37))                                          |
| Attack a ball entirely above the net | From anywhere             | Only taking off from behind the attack line ([rule 13.2](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf#page=35)) |
| Serve                                | No                        | Only the player in zone 1                                                                                                                                    |

Whether you are front or back row depends on your zone at the moment of the serve, not where you run to afterwards. A back-row player who sneaks to the net after the serve still may not complete a block.
